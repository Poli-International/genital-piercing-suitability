## What is the Genital Piercing Anatomy Suitability Checker?

The Genital Piercing Anatomy Suitability Checker is a free, browser-based educational reference for **14 genital piercing placements** across female, male, and shared/perineal anatomy. It is not a diagnostic tool and it does not return a digital verdict. Instead, it explains, placement by placement, the anatomical conditions a professional piercer looks for during an in-person physical examination.

For each placement, the tool presents:

- **Required anatomy and reasoning**, the tissue folds, depth, and dimensions a placement depends on.
- **In-person assessment (what the studio checks)**, the manual tests a piercer performs, such as the cotton-swab test used to evaluate clitoral hood depth for a VCH.
- **Common reasons a studio refuses the placement**, the anatomical contraindications that lead a professional to decline.
- **Typical healing time**, so expectations are realistic.
- **Starting jewellery**, the gauge and format a placement is typically first fitted with.
- **Four key questions to ask your piercer**, to structure the consultation conversation.

The tool also includes a private consultation worksheet with reflection prompts, a free-text notes field, an optional browser-save checkbox, and a print-ready consultation sheet. Everything runs locally in the browser, and the interface is available in seven languages: English, French, German, Spanish, Italian, Dutch, and Portuguese.

A shared clinical section at the bottom of the page lists the five general physical reasons a piercer says no: insufficient tissue depth, immediate nerve proximity, high-friction or pressure points, pre-existing scar tissue, and active skin irritation.

## How to Use the Checker

### 1. Switch the Interface Language

Open the **Language** selector in the top bar and choose from English, French, Italian, German, Spanish, Dutch, or Portuguese. All labels, prompts, criteria, and diagram captions update without a page reload.

### 2. Read the Age and Consent Notice

The banner at the top states that this is an adult educational reference (18+), and that piercers require government identification and informed adult consent prior to consultation.

### 3. Search by Placement or Common Name

Click into the search field with the placeholder **"Search placement or common name (e.g. VCH, clit hood, PA, guiche)..."** and type an anatomical term, acronym, or colloquial name. Use the **Clear search** button to reset the filter and show all placements again.

### 4. Filter by Anatomy Category and Select a Placement

Open the **Filter by anatomy** dropdown and choose one of four options: **All placements (14)**, **Female anatomy placements**, **Male anatomy placements**, or **Shared / perineal placements**. Then use the adjacent **Select placement** dropdown to load a specific piercing's reference card.

### 5. Read the Placement Anatomy Reference Card

The active placement card presents the required anatomy and reasoning, the in-person assessment steps, the common reasons for refusal, the typical healing time, the starting jewellery, and the four key questions to ask your piercer.

### 6. Show or Hide the Schematic Diagram

Below the placement overview, click **Show schematic diagram** to view a neutral, non-explicit vector cross-section showing the piercing channel, tissue layers, and entry/exit points. Click **Hide schematic diagram** to close the illustration when reading in public.

### 7. Complete the "Is It Right for Me?" Consultation Worksheet

Scroll to the interactive worksheet. Reflect on the guided prompts covering **comfort with in-person physical assessment** and **time and activity flexibility for healing**. Type personal notes or medical questions into the **My private notes & questions for consultation** field. Tick **Save private notes in this browser** to keep notes on this device between visits, or click **Clear all private notes** to empty the field and the local storage at any time.

### 8. Print the Consultation Sheet

Click **Print Consultation Sheet** at the bottom of the worksheet. A print-specific stylesheet hides navigation bars, the language selector, search fields, and buttons, producing a clean grayscale summary containing the selected placement, the assessment criteria, the key questions, and your notes. Print directly or choose "Save as PDF" in your browser's print dialog.

### 9. Review the Shared Clinical Reasons for Refusal

Scroll to the shared clinical reference section at the end of the page to read the five primary physical reasons a professional declines a placement: insufficient tissue depth, immediate nerve proximity, high mechanical friction or pressure points, pre-existing scar tissue, and active skin irritation.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Can I determine my genital piercing suitability online?**
No website or photograph can reliably confirm whether your individual anatomy suits a genital piercing. A professional piercer must palpate the tissue in person to assess thickness, elasticity, mobility, and depth, and to check blood vessel and nerve paths under direct lighting. Final suitability can only be established during an in-person examination in a professional studio.

**What does the cotton-swab test for a VCH piercing involve?**
The cotton-swab test is a manual assessment a piercer uses to check whether the clitoral hood is deep enough for a VCH. The padded tip of a sterile swab is gently slid under the hood to confirm a bar can sit comfortably without pressing on the clitoral glans. If the swab does not glide in freely or the hood does not fully cover the cotton tip, the anatomy does not allow this vertical placement.

**Why does a Prince Albert piercing require a thick starting gauge?**
A Prince Albert must be pierced at a minimum of 12 gauge (2.0 mm) or 10 gauge (2.4 mm) to prevent the cheese-cutter effect. Jewellery that is too thin concentrates mechanical pull on a tiny area of the delicate urethral tissue, which can cause painful cutting or migration. A robust gauge distributes pressure evenly and supports stable healing.

**Why do studios frequently refuse a Christina piercing?**
The Christina is a surface piercing on the mons pubis whose lower exit point sits at the upper edge of the clitoral hood. Studios decline it when the client lacks a sufficiently pronounced natural skin fold in which the jewellery base can rest protected. Without enough tissue depth, constant friction from waistbands and everyday movement almost inevitably leads to rejection.

**Can an uncircumcised person get a Dydoe piercing?**
Dydoe piercings pass through the ridge of the glans and are rarely recommended for uncircumcised people. The natural foreskin covers the glans ridge and traps moisture, sebum, and bacteria around the healing wound. The constant sliding of the foreskin also creates ongoing shear forces on the jewellery, causing chronic irritation and a high migration risk.

**What is the anatomical difference between an Apadravya and an Ampallang?**
An Apadravya runs vertically through the centre of the glans, entering from below through the urethra and exiting on the top surface. An Ampallang crosses the glans horizontally from side to side, intersecting the urethra in the middle. Both are demanding trans-glandular procedures that require substantial tissue volume and expert technique to spare deeper vascular structures.

**Does the tool store or send my notes anywhere?**
No. The application sends no data over the network and transmits neither notes nor search terms to Poli International or any external server. Without the **Save private notes in this browser** checkbox ticked, notes exist only in the browser tab's memory and are lost on close or refresh. If you tick the box, the text is stored in your browser's local storage under the key `poli_genital_suitability_notes`. Clicking **Clear all private notes** empties the field and deletes the key immediately.

**What does the printed consultation sheet contain?**
The print output contains only the name of the selected placement, the four consultation questions, and the notes you typed. A print-specific stylesheet hides navigation bars, the language selector, search fields, and buttons, producing a clean grayscale document suitable for A4 or Letter paper.

**Is the tool available in languages other than English?**
Yes. The interface is available in seven languages: English, French, German, Spanish, Italian, Dutch, and Portuguese. Switching the language selector translates all labels, prompts, criteria, and diagram captions without reloading the page.

**Does this tool replace an in-person consultation?**
No. It is strictly educational and provides no medical diagnoses or digital suitability verdicts. It cannot feel tissue elasticity, measure differences between flaccid and erect states, locate deep vessels, or assess individual wound healing. Only an experienced piercer, after a thorough manual examination in a hygienic studio, can decide whether a placement is viable.
